## Summary

Lettuce grows best in cool weather. At low elevations in Hawaiʻi, CTAHR recommends
heat-tolerant semi-head types such as ‘UH Manoa’ (a selection of ‘Green Mignonette’),
which can be grown year-round, though heads are larger and tip-burn and bolting are
less of a problem in cooler weather or at higher elevations. True head lettuces succeed
reliably only at higher, cooler sites (for example Kula, Volcano or Kamuela).

## Timing

Sow or transplant year-round if you are growing a heat-tolerant leaf or semi-head
type. Protect plants from heavy rain, which bruises soft leaves. Harvest leaves as the
row is thinned.

## Spacing and water

CTAHR sows leaf or semi-head seed ¼–½ inch deep in rows 8–10 inches apart, then thins
to 8–10 inches in the row. Head types, where they can be grown, are sown in rows
12–15 inches apart. The soil should not dry out: high temperature and drought cause
tip-burn. Irrigate in the morning so foliage can dry.
