## Summary

Lettuce is a cool-season crop that suits a mild oceanic climate if it is sown
little and often. The Royal Horticultural Society says a sunny site is usually
best, with some light shade in the middle of summer so heat does not trigger
premature flowering (bolting). Keep plants evenly watered.

## Sowing

- For summer and autumn harvests, sow outdoors from late March to late July. For
  an earlier crop, sow indoors in early February and plant out in early March
  under cloches.
- For early winter harvests, sow outdoors in early August and cover from late
  September, or grow under glass.
- For spring harvests, sow a winter variety in September or October, in a
  greenhouse or, in mild areas, under cloches or in a cold frame.
- Sow about 1 cm deep, in rows 30 cm apart, and thin to 15–30 cm. Space larger
  hearting plants about 30 cm apart and smaller loose-leaf plants about 15 cm
  apart. Sow a short row every fortnight to avoid a glut.

## Harvest

RHS says lettuces can be harvested from late spring through to winter if sown
regularly with suitable seasonal varieties. Loose-leaf types may be ready in
four to six weeks; hearting types take around 10 to 14 weeks. Cut hearting
lettuces when a firm heart has formed; loose-leaf types can be picked
cut-and-come-again once leaves are about 10 cm long.
