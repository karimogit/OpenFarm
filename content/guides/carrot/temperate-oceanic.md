## Summary

Carrots are a direct-sown crop for sun and light, well-drained soil. The Royal
Horticultural Society says they are fairly drought-resistant in the ground and
need regular watering mainly in containers or in long dry spells. Sow small
batches from early spring for a long harvest.

## Sowing

- Sow directly where they are to grow; transplanting can fork the roots.
- Early varieties can be sown in February or March under cloches or fleece. The
  main sowing season is April to early July. Sow about 1 cm deep, as thinly as
  possible, with 15–30 cm between rows, and thin to 5–7.5 cm.
- Successional sowings every three to four weeks spread the harvest. Late sowings
  after mid-May avoid the first generation of carrot fly; lifting before late
  August avoids the second, though covering with insect-proof mesh is the method
  RHS research found most effective.

## Harvest

Roots are typically ready about 12–16 weeks after sowing. Baby carrots may be
lifted after a month or two. Main-season carrots can be harvested through summer
and into early autumn, and can be left in the ground into winter and lifted as
needed, with straw or cardboard if frost is a risk. If the soil lies very wet,
RHS recommends lifting and storing instead.
