## Summary

Apples are well suited to mild oceanic climates. Choose a sunny, sheltered spot that is not prone to
late frosts, which damage the blossom, in fertile soil that drains freely.

## Choosing trees

- You need a second variety flowering at the same time nearby, or a self-fertile variety.
- Pick a rootstock for your space: dwarfing (M9, M26) for small gardens, pots and cordons;
  semi-dwarfing (MM106) for trees up to about 4 m.
- Modern varieties are often more disease resistant than old ones; look for RHS AGM varieties.

## Planting

- Plant while dormant, **autumn to spring**. Bare-root trees are sold late autumn to early spring.
- Stake free-standing trees and keep a circle at least 1 m across free of grass.
- In pots, use a dwarfing rootstock and a container at least 45 cm across with loam-based compost.

## Care

- Water new trees in dry spells for the first couple of years; established trees rarely need it.
- Mulch each spring. Thin fruit in early July for bigger apples, and remove fruit in the first year
  after planting.
- Prune free-standing trees in **winter** and trained forms (cordon, espalier, fan) in **late summer**.

## Harvest and storage

Pick when fruit has sized up, coloured and comes away with a gentle twist. Early apples keep only a
few days; later varieties store for months in a cool, frost-free, airy place.
