## Summary

Ordinary apples such as 'Golden Delicious', 'Gala' or 'Fuji' do not get enough winter cold here.
Low-chill cultivars needing about **250–300 chill hours** – 'Anna', 'Dorsett Golden' and
'TropicSweet' – crop in north Florida home gardens.

## Planting

- Plant healthy 1–2-year-old bare-root trees from **late December to February**, so roots establish
  before spring growth. Container trees can go in all year if watered through dry spells.
- Full sun; hilltops are better than low spots for air movement and drainage. Aim for soil pH 6–6.5.
- Plant at least two of the recommended cultivars – they pollinate each other.
- These are spur-type trees and stay fairly small even on seedling rootstock; M7A and MM106 did well in
  a Florida trial.

## Care

- Keep 1.2–1.8 m (4–6 ft) around the trunk weed-free; mulch, but not against the trunk.
- Fertilise in January–March and again in June. Zinc and boron deficiencies are common.
- Water newly planted trees often in dry weather; older trees weekly or every two weeks.

## Bloom and harvest

Low-chill apples bloom over a long period in late winter (usually late February), so later flowers
often escape frost. Fruit ripens **late May through June** and keeps six to eight weeks.
