## Summary

Garlic is easy and hardy. Most varieties need **one to two months of cold**
(below about 10 °C) to split into cloves, so autumn planting works best.

## Planting

- Plant from **mid-autumn to early winter** (October–December). Some varieties can
  be planted in late winter (February–March), though bulbs are often smaller.
- Plant individual cloves flat end down, tip **2.5 cm** below the surface,
  **15 cm** apart in rows **30 cm** apart.
- Use bulbs sold for planting rather than supermarket garlic.
- In heavy, wet soil plant on a ridge or raised bed, or start cloves in modules in
  a cold frame and plant out in spring.

## Care

Keep weed-free – garlic competes poorly. Water in dry spells during spring.

## Harvest

Lift when the leaves start to yellow, usually **June–August** for autumn-planted
garlic. Dry the bulbs in a warm, airy place before storing.

## Problems

Rust (orange spots on leaves), white rot and onion fly. Rotate with other alliums.
