## Summary

Garlic overwinters well under snow or mulch. Choose **cold-hardy (often hardneck)
varieties**; many commercial softneck types from mild climates grow poorly here.

## Planting

- Plant cloves in autumn, **one to two weeks after the first killing frost**.
  Roots form before the ground freezes; shoots usually appear in spring.
- Base of the clove 5–8 cm below the surface, cloves 15 cm apart.
- Cover with **7–10 cm of straw or leaf mulch** to protect against fluctuating
  winter temperatures.

## Care

- Garlic needs a well-drained, moisture-retentive soil with pH 6.0–7.0.
- Control weeds early. Water about 2.5 cm per week in dry spells; stop two weeks
  before harvest.
- Remove the flower stalks (scapes) of hardneck types – they are edible.

## Harvest

Usually **July** (late June to late July in Minnesota, possibly later in the north)
when the lower leaves are brown but about half the upper leaves are still green.
Cure for 3–4 weeks in a warm, dry, airy place.
