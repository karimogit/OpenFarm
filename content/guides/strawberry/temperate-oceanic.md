## Summary

Easy and rewarding. Combine summer-fruiting and perpetual types for berries from early summer into
autumn.

## Planting

- Plant in **spring, late summer or early autumn**; August–September planting gives plants time to
  establish before fruiting next year.
- Fertile, well-drained, slightly acidic soil in a sheltered, sunny spot away from frost pockets.
  Dig in well-rotted organic matter a few weeks before planting.
- Set the base of the crown at soil level – too deep rots, too shallow dries out. Space **35–40 cm**
  apart in rows **75 cm** apart (about 20 cm in containers).

## Care

- Water during dry spells, at the base and in the morning, keeping the crown and fruit dry.
- Put straw or mats under developing fruit; net against birds; fleece flowers if frost threatens.
- After summer-fruiting types finish, cut old leaves back to about 10 cm above the crown.
- Replant every three or four years in a new spot.

## Harvest

Summer-fruiting types crop for two to three weeks between early June and early August; perpetual
types from early summer to early autumn. Pick fully red berries in the warmest part of the day.
Cloches bring crops at least a week earlier, an unheated greenhouse about two weeks.
