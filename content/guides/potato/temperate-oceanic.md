## Summary

The cool, moist oceanic climate is close to ideal for potatoes. The main risks
are late spring frost on young shoots and late blight in warm, wet summers.

## Chitting (pre-sprouting)

Optional but worthwhile for early varieties: set seed potatoes eyes-up in a
bright, frost-free place for 4–6 weeks until shoots are about 2 cm long. Start
in late January (mild areas) to early March (colder spots).

## Planting

Use certified seed potatoes and plant in 15 cm deep trenches:

| Type | When | Spacing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First earlies | around late March | 30 cm apart, rows 60 cm |
| Second earlies | early to mid-April | 30 cm apart, rows 60 cm |
| Maincrop | mid-April to early May | 37 cm apart, rows 75 cm |

Plant slightly later in colder or northern areas.

## Earthing up

When shoots are about 23 cm tall, draw soil up around them, leaving the top 10 cm
showing. Repeat a few times. This keeps tubers covered (green tubers are toxic)
and protects shoots from frost.

## Harvest

- First earlies: **June–July**, when flowers open or buds drop.
- Second earlies: **July–August**.
- Maincrop: **late August to October**; cut the foliage down if blight appears and
  lift 2–3 weeks later.

## Problems

Late blight, potato cyst nematodes, slugs and scab. Rotate crops – don't grow
potatoes (or tomatoes) in the same ground year after year.
