## Summary

Potatoes tolerate cool soil and are planted early in spring. Rich, well-drained soil and steady
moisture make the biggest difference in a dry climate.

## Planting

- Plant as soon as the soil can be worked and has reached about 7 °C (45 °F), and no more than two
  to three weeks before the last spring frost unless plants are protected.
- Use certified seed potatoes. Pieces of about 50 g (an egg-sized tuber) with at least one eye work
  well; warm them indoors for a week so they start to sprout.
- Plant **8–10 cm deep**, **30–38 cm** apart, in rows **60–90 cm** apart (or the same equal spacing
  in a raised bed).

## Care

- Start hilling soil around the stems about two weeks after emergence and finish by flowering, or use
  straw mulch, so tubers are not exposed to light and turn green.
- Potatoes are heavy feeders; running out of nitrogen by August is the most common problem.
  Avoid heavy applications of fresh manure, which encourage scab.
- Too wet and tubers rot; too dry and they become knobbly. Keep moisture steady.
- Rotate where you grow potatoes.

## Harvest

Typically about 125 days after planting for main-crop types; earlier cultivars sooner.
