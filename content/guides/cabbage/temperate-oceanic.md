## Summary

Cabbages are hardy brassicas for a sunny site with firm, fertile, free-draining
soil. The Royal Horticultural Society groups them by harvesting season — spring,
summer, autumn and winter — grown in the same way but sown at different times.
Cover the crop against caterpillars and pigeons, and rotate brassicas.

## Sowing and planting

- Summer types: sow from late winter to mid-spring (February–April outdoors;
  protect early sowings) and harvest in mid- to late summer.
- Autumn and winter types: sow April–June and harvest before winter (autumn) or
  through the lean months (winter).
- Spring types: sow in July for overwintering; pick as spring greens or leave to
  head from mid-spring to mid-summer.
- Transplant when plants are 10–15 cm tall, setting them deeply. Space compact
  varieties about 30 cm apart and larger ones up to 45 cm.

## Harvest

Plants generally take about four to six months. Cut when a firm head has reached
the size you want. A selection of types can be harvested all year; one sowing
gives only its own season.
