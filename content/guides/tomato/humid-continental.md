## Summary

A short frost-free season is the main constraint. Long summer days help, but
nights are cool. Choose **early, short-season or cold-tolerant varieties**
(cherry and bush types are usually most reliable) and use any shelter you have:
a greenhouse, polytunnel, south-facing wall or cold frame.

## Sowing

- Start seeds indoors about **5–6 weeks before transplanting** – in practice
  mid-April for a June planting. Earlier sowing gives leggy plants and does not
  necessarily mean an earlier harvest.
- Germinate warm (24–29 °C with a heat mat), then grow on at around 21 °C under
  bright light.

## Planting out

- Transplant only after all danger of frost has passed and the soil has warmed,
  which in much of Sweden is **early to mid-June** (earlier in a greenhouse).
- Stake or cage at planting time. Black plastic or a fleece cover can warm the soil
  in the first weeks.

## Harvest

Expect ripe fruit from **August**. Pick everything – including green fruit – before
the first autumn frost (often September) and ripen it indoors.

## Problems

- Cool nights below about 10–13 °C reduce fruit set; a greenhouse helps a lot.
- Rotate: avoid places where tomatoes, potatoes, peppers or aubergines grew in the
  last 3–4 years.
