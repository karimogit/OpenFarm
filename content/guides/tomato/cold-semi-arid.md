## Summary

Dry air, strong sun and late spring frosts define the season. Tomatoes need warm nights and warm
soil, so planting too early gains nothing – plants just sit and "pout".

## Sowing

Start seed indoors **6–8 weeks before planting out**.

## Planting out

- Typically **late May** to the first part of **June**, after the last frost (Front Range average
  15 May) and when soil is at least 13 °C, ideally 16–18 °C (60–65 °F).
- Wait until nights stay above about 11 °C (52 °F) and days above 16 °C (60 °F). A week of cool days
  below 13 °C stunts plants.
- Black plastic mulch warms the soil and can push production two to three weeks earlier; water-filled
  plant protectors ("walls of water") guard against mild frost.
- Lay leggy transplants horizontally in a shallow trench so they root along the stem.

## Care

- Mulch to save water, keep moisture even (reducing blossom-end rot) and limit early-blight splash.
- In July and August, nights fall below 13 °C about half the time, which affects fruit set.

## Varieties

CSU names Big Beef, Celebrity, Early Girl and Medina as consistent performers. Where the season is
short, choose early cultivars that mature in about 50 days.

## Harvest

From midsummer until the first autumn frost (Front Range average 15 October).
