## Summary

CTAHR reports that tomato can be grown throughout the year in most of Hawaiʻi if the variety
is adapted to the site. Bush (determinate) and vining (indeterminate) types are both used.
Most varieties mature in 60–80 days. Warm, wet weather makes fungal and bacterial leaf spots
and blights common.

## Timing

There is no single “season” in the leaflet: sow and transplant year-round, raising seedlings
in cells or pots and moving them out 3–5 weeks after sowing. Harvest can begin when the
bottom of the fruit shows some pink; fruit picked about three-quarters ripe will finish
indoors. One or two harvests a week are typical.

## Spacing and support

CTAHR spaces seedlings 15 inches (38 cm) apart in rows 24–30 inches (61–76 cm) apart.
Keeping plants off the ground on a stake, cage or trellis reduces disease and slug damage.
Indeterminate hybrids are pruned and trellised; determinate bush cultivars need not be.

## Water

Irrigate two to three times a week before fruit set when rain is scarce, and three to four
times a week after fruit set if the weather is dry. Water the soil, not the leaves.
