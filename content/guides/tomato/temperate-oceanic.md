## Summary

Tomatoes are frost-tender and need a long, warm season. In cool, cloudy oceanic
summers they crop well outdoors in a sheltered, sunny spot, and even better under
glass or a polytunnel. Late blight (*Phytophthora infestans*) is the main limiting
factor in wet summers.

## Sowing

- Sow indoors at about 18 °C. For outdoor crops sow **late March to early April**
  (sow earlier, late February to mid-March, only if plants will go into a greenhouse).
- Pot on seedlings once the first true leaves appear and keep them in bright light.

## Planting out

- Harden off and plant outdoors only after the last frost, usually **late May to
  early June** in the Netherlands and lowland UK.
- Choose your warmest, most sheltered spot. Space plants **45–60 cm** apart.
- Cordon (indeterminate) types need a stake and side-shoot removal; bush types do not.

## Care

- Water regularly and deeply; irregular watering causes blossom-end rot and split fruit.
  Water at the base rather than over the leaves.
- Feed with a high-potash fertiliser once the first truss has set.

## Harvest

Pick fruit as it colours, typically **July to October**. Before the first autumn
frost, bring green fruit indoors to ripen.

## Problems

- **Late blight**: brown patches on leaves and stems in warm, wet weather. Grow
  blight-resistant varieties, give plants space and air, and avoid wetting foliage.
- Keep tomatoes and potatoes apart where possible – they share blight and other
  diseases. (This is why tomato and potato are listed as companions to avoid.)

> Companion planting notes (basil, marigold) are traditional garden lore, not
> well-established research results.
