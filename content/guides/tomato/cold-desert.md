## Summary

UNR Extension describes northern Nevada as having three vegetable seasons: early
spring, summer and early fall, and a relatively short frost-free window. For
Reno it uses an average last spring frost of 15 May and an earliest fall frost of
15 September, and it recommends planning on about 90 frost-free days.

Tomato is listed among the warm-season, frost-tender crops that should be planted
out after the last frost.

## Sowing and planting

- Start transplants indoors from mid-March through mid-April, or buy nursery
  plants.
- Plant out after the last frost, in May to early June in the Reno area. Earlier
  setting out is only suggested with protection such as hotcaps or row covers.
- Choose a site with full sun for at least six to eight hours a day, away from
  buildings and shade trees, and with a reliable water supply.

## Water

The fact sheet stresses a consistent water supply in this dry climate: plants
should not dry out or sit in waterlogged soil. Drip irrigation is commonly used
there because it cuts evaporation and waters the root zone.

## Harvest

The fact sheet does not give tomato harvest months. With a mid-May to early-June
planting and a mid-September frost, fruit is expected in the short summer, listed
here as July–September on that basis.
