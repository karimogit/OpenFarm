## Summary

In the wet–dry tropics, tomatoes are grown in the **cooler, drier part of the
year**. In South Florida, UF/IFAS recommends planting from **August to February**.
Plants set in August–September face more pests and diseases that thrive in hot,
wet weather; mid-season plantings are usually easiest.

## Planting

- Use transplants about 4–6 weeks old. UF/IFAS lists 70–90 days from transplant to
  harvest (90–110 from seed) for supported tomatoes.
- Spacing: 18–32 inches (about 45–80 cm) between plants and 48 inches (about 120 cm)
  between rows for staked plants.
- Choose heat- and disease-tolerant varieties suited to your region.

## Care

- Stake or cage plants; good airflow reduces fungal and bacterial disease.
- Mulch and irrigate during the dry season; avoid waterlogging.

## Harvest

Roughly **November to May** in South Florida, depending on planting date.

## Problems

Bacterial spot, early blight, whiteflies and the viruses they spread, and
root-knot nematodes are common in warm, humid lowlands. Rotation and resistant
varieties help.
