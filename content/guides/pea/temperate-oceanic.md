## Summary

Peas are a cool-season crop for a sunny, sheltered, well-drained site. The
Royal Horticultural Society distinguishes garden (shelling) peas from mangetouts
and sugarsnaps; all are grown in a similar way. Sow successive batches, or both
early and maincrop varieties, for harvests through the summer.

## Sowing and planting

- Sow indoors in February or March, especially if the soil is still cold and
  wet, and plant out in March and April once seedlings are about 20 cm tall.
- The main outdoor sowing season is March to June, once the soil is about 10 °C.
  RHS classes garden-pea varieties as first earlies (sow March to early June,
  harvest from about 12 weeks), second earlies (March to June, 13–14 weeks) and
  maincrops (March to June, 14–16 weeks).
- Sow about 5 cm deep and 7.5 cm apart. Taller varieties go in a single or
  double row with 30 cm between the two lines; shorter varieties can be sown in
  a 15 cm-wide trench as a staggered triple row.

## Harvest

RHS says that by choosing different types and varieties you can harvest from
June through to October. Pick regularly so the plants keep flowering. Shelling
peas are ready when the pods are well filled; mangetouts and sugarsnaps should
be picked at about 7.5 cm long, just as the peas start to develop.
