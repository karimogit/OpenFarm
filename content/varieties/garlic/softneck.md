Softneck garlic usually does not produce a stiff scape. Colorado State University Extension says it generally stores longer than hardneck, has more cloves per bulb (the 2018 fact sheet gives a typical range of 10–40 smaller cloves), and is more often the type sold in shops. It is described as doing better in warmer climates and as more forgiving of milder conditions, though CSU notes that both types can be grown in western Colorado with the right planting time and winter protection.

CSU refers to softneck garlic as “subspecies sativum”. That is recorded here as CSU’s horticultural grouping, not as a botanical rank we have independently accepted (GRIN Taxonomy lists *Allium sativum* var. *sativum*). The scientific name on this page stays *Allium sativum*.
