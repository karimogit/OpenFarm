Hardneck garlic sends up a flower stalk (scape) in early summer. Colorado State University Extension describes it as more cold-hardy than softneck, with fewer but larger cloves and more vivid, complex flavour in cool growing conditions. The scapes are usually cut off so the plant puts energy into the bulb; the scapes themselves are eaten.

CSU refers to hardneck garlic as “subspecies ophioscorodon”. That wording is recorded here as CSU’s horticultural grouping, not as a botanical rank we have independently accepted (GRIN Taxonomy lists *Allium sativum* var. *ophioscorodon*). The scientific name on this page stays *Allium sativum*.
