## What it's for

A shovel has a dished blade for lifting and throwing soil, compost or mulch, unlike a flat-bladed spade used mainly for digging and edging. Rounded-point shovels are common for garden digging and moving loose material.

## How to use it

Push the blade into the pile or soil with your foot, then lift and throw. For a planting hole, cut around the outline and lift out the soil; do not use a shovel as a pry bar on rocks, which can bend the blade or snap the handle.

## Care and maintenance

Clean and dry after use. Sharpen the leading edge if it dulls, and oil metal and wood before long storage (Iowa State University Extension; University of Wisconsin–Madison Extension).
